The problem

AI trust doesn't stop at the model boundary.

A prompt can travel through applications, gateways, routers, inference providers and downstream infrastructure. A headline privacy promise does not automatically describe every hop. We want to make those paths more observable and the evidence more reproducible.

01 / TRACE

Understand the route

Record declared providers, models, endpoints, routers and regions so an experiment has supply-chain context.

02 / TEST

Use controlled canaries

Plant unique synthetic markers in authorised test prompts and observe whether controlled callbacks occur.

03 / EVIDENCE

Correlate what happened

Build structured timelines from request metadata, callbacks and sandbox audit events.

Research methodology

Instrument. Observe. Correlate. Report.

A repeatable experiment lifecycle designed to separate direct observations from assumptions and conclusions.

STEP 01

Instrument

Define scope, generate synthetic canaries and inspect outbound data locally.

STEP 02

Observe

Send controlled requests and monitor callback infrastructure you own.

STEP 03

Correlate

Join timestamps, experiment IDs, route metadata and sandbox audit evidence.

STEP 04

Report

Publish reproducible findings with confidence levels and explicit limitations.

Research principles

Evidence without overclaiming.

Synthetic data only. No production credentials, customer prompts or real personal data.
Authorised testing. No unsolicited probing of providers or infrastructure.
Least privilege. Isolated accounts and inert credential-shaped markers.
Conservative findings. A callback is evidence of access, not proof of malicious intent or credential theft.
Privacy by design. Minimise raw prompt collection and prefer redacted metadata.
Honest limitations. External tests cannot conclusively prove that no copy was retained downstream.
Research provenance

Building on published security research.

AI Trust Observatory is inspired by Dragos's investigation by HD Moore, “Three breaches, endless leaks: Your AI supply chain needs canaries” (19 November 2026). The investigation used unique canaries across approximately two million AI prompts to examine provider compromises, reseller access and downstream data handling.

This is an independent research and engineering project. Dragos and HD Moore are not authors, maintainers, sponsors or endorsers of AI Trust Observatory. The project does not claim to reproduce Dragos's proprietary tooling or independently validate its findings.
